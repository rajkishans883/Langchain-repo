What is a Black Hole?
A black hole is a region of spacetime where gravity is so extremely strong that nothing — not even light — can escape once it crosses a certain boundary called the event horizon.
The name "black hole" was popularized by physicist John Wheeler in 1967. Before that, they were called "frozen stars" or "dark stars."
How Do Black Holes Form?
The most common way:

A massive star (usually >8–20 times the mass of our Sun) runs out of fuel.
It can no longer resist its own gravity.
The core collapses in a fraction of a second.
If the remaining core is massive enough (typically >3 solar masses), it collapses past the neutron star stage into a black hole.

This is often accompanied by a supernova explosion, though some black holes form more quietly through direct collapse.
Other formation methods:

Supermassive black holes (millions to billions of solar masses) at the centers of galaxies — their formation is still not fully understood (possibly from merging smaller black holes + gas accretion in the early universe).
Primordial black holes (hypothetical tiny ones formed during the Big Bang).

Key Parts of a Black Hole

Event Horizon
The "point of no return." Once you cross it, escape is impossible.
For a non-rotating black hole, the radius of the event horizon is the Schwarzschild radius:$$r_s = \frac{2GM}{c^2}$$
$  G  $ = gravitational constant
$  M  $ = mass of the black hole
$  c  $ = speed of light
Examples:
1 solar mass black hole → ~3 km radius
10 solar mass → ~30 km
Sagittarius A* (our galaxy's central black hole, 4.3 million solar masses) → ~12.7 million km (~0.09 AU)

Singularity
At the very center, all the mass is crushed into a point of infinite density and zero volume according to general relativity. This is where physics as we know it breaks down.
Accretion Disk (often visible)
Gas and dust spiraling into the black hole heat up to millions of degrees and glow brightly in X-rays.

Real Properties

They don't "suck" everything in: They have gravity like any other object of the same mass. If you replaced the Sun with a black hole of the same mass, Earth's orbit wouldn't change.
Spaghettification: Near small black holes, tidal forces stretch you vertically and compress you horizontally like spaghetti before you cross the horizon.
Time dilation: Time slows dramatically near the event horizon (from an outside observer's view, you appear to freeze).
Hawking Radiation: Stephen Hawking showed black holes slowly evaporate by emitting radiation due to quantum effects near the horizon. Smaller black holes evaporate faster.

Major Discoveries

1915: Einstein's General Relativity predicts them.
1930s: Oppenheimer and others work out the theory.
1970s: Cygnus X-1 becomes the first strong candidate.
2015: LIGO detects gravitational waves from black hole merger (first direct proof).
2019: Event Horizon Telescope takes the first image of a black hole's shadow (M87*).
2022: Image of Sagittarius A* (our own galaxy's black hole).

Mind-Blowing Facts

Black holes can spin (Kerr black holes) and have charge (Reissner-Nordström), but according to the No-Hair Theorem, those are the only three properties they can have (mass, spin, charge).
The universe's largest known black holes are over 60–100 billion solar masses.
Information paradox: What happens to information that falls into a black hole? This is one of the biggest unsolved problems in physics (involves quantum mechanics + gravity).
